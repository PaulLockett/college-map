The short answer first

IB is a harder version of high school that ends with a diploma the whole world recognizes.

IB stands for International Baccalaureate. It's a program run out of Switzerland that thousands of high schools around the world teach the same way — so an IB diploma from Mobile means the same thing as one from Tokyo or Berlin.

Davidson is one of those schools. The actual IB Diploma is junior and senior year. Before that, 9th and 10th grade are "pre-IB" — regular Davidson classes, but the harder versions, arranged to get you ready.

It is not a different school. It's a track inside the school you're already going to.

What makes it different from normal classes

Every high school has hard classes. IB adds three things no normal track has — and these three are the whole personality of the program.

TOK — Theory of KnowledgeAn entire class about how you know things are true. Not facts — the question underneath the facts. You spend a year arguing about what counts as evidence, and it's graded.
CAS — Creativity, Activity, ServiceYou have to actually do things outside class: make something creative, move your body, and volunteer. It's not extra credit. Skip it and you don't get the diploma, no matter your grades.
EE — the Extended EssayA 4,000-word research paper on a question you pick, written over two years with a teacher advising you. Basically a small college thesis, in high school.
Why this matters more than it sounds. AP is a set of separate tests. IB is one connected program that asks you to research, argue, and do — which is why it looks different on a college application even when the class list looks similar.

How the scoring works

6 subjects, each scored 1–7up to 42
TOK + Extended Essay, graded togetherup to 3
Perfect score45
What you need to earn the diploma24+

Of your six subjects, three are Higher Level (about 240 hours over two years) and three are Standard Level (about 150 hours). You also have to finish CAS. There's no way to buy your way past it with a high average.

How you actually get in Davidson

There's a January application.Davidson's own admissions policy says students in 8th or 9th grade apply through a district application in January, to start the IB program in 9th or 10th grade. Decisions go out in March.
They screen on three things.Your grades from middle school through right now — plus your discipline record and your attendance record. Two of those three are entirely about showing up and not getting in trouble.
There's a second door in 11th grade.The IB Coordinator meets with interested 10th graders in January, holds a parent info meeting in February, and students sign a Statement of Intent. Generally they look for B or higher in everything — but they say the review is holistic, and students with a record of taking hard classes get in.
Who to talk to.The IB Coordinator at Davidson is Ashley Cauley. There's also a $250 program fee.
If you're zoned somewhere else.Mobile County's Signature Academy transfer application closes December 31 and lets you pick up to three schools — but the district does not provide transportation for Signature Academy transfers.
So — did you miss it? No. If you're starting 9th grade now, the January application is still ahead of you, and the 11th-grade door is ahead of that. There are two on-ramps left, and the thing that determines whether you make it through either one is the grades, attendance, and discipline record you start building on day one.

Things worth knowing before you decide

You have to choose between IB and AP.

Not at Davidson. The school says 100% of its IB class of 2024 took three or more AP classes, and all of them were named AP Scholars. Most of them were in the EPIC engineering program too.

IB is just harder classes.

The classes are harder, but the real difference is TOK, CAS, and the Extended Essay — a philosophy class, a do-things requirement, and a research paper. That's what you're actually signing up for.

You can pick any language.

At Davidson the IB language options are German, French, or Spanish. Worth knowing early if you had a different one in mind — it's a fair question to put to the coordinator.

The diploma is automatic if your grades are good.

You need 24+ points, no score of 1, and completed CAS. Skipping the volunteering and the creative hours disqualifies you no matter how well you test.

Colleges don't care.

Davidson says IB is recognized by around 3,300 universities in 90 countries. Most US colleges give actual credit for Higher Level exams — though how much varies a lot by school, so it's worth checking specific ones later.

What to do about it this year

Four things, and two of them are free.

The application isn't until January. What it looks at starts now.

  1. Show up and stay out of trouble. Attendance and discipline are literally two of the three screening criteria. This is the rare case where the easy thing and the important thing are the same thing.
  2. Ask a counselor about your math placement in the first month. Geometry in 9th grade is the hinge the whole four-year plan turns on.
  3. Ask to be put in the hardest classes available now, whether or not you end up applying to IB. Pre-IB is mostly honors and AP anyway, so this is the same move either way.
  4. Email the IB Coordinator and say you're interested. Being a name they already know in January is worth more than it sounds, and it costs one email.
